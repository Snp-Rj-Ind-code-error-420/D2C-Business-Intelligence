# 1. Dashboard One: Financial Performance Review

**Objective:** Help the founder understand financial performance, identify profitable segments, and spot revenue opportunities.

## A. KPI Cards — Top Row

I'd use six KPI cards, arranged in two rows of three if the screen is not wide enough.

| KPI | What It Measures | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Total Net Revenue** | Revenue after discounts, as calculated in your notebook | Measures sales performance |
| **Total Gross Profit** | Net revenue minus product cost | Shows profit generated before other operating expenses |
| **Overall Gross Margin** | Gross profit ÷ net revenue × 100 | Measures profitability relative to revenue |
| **Average Order Value (AOV)** | Total net revenue ÷ unique orders | Measures average revenue per order |
| **Total Orders** | Number of unique orders | Shows transaction volume |
| **Revenue Growth (%)** | Revenue growth compared with a comparable previous period | Shows whether the business is growing |

### Suggested comparison

Each KPI should ideally display:

- Current selected period's value.
- Change compared with the previous equivalent period.
- A small indicator showing whether the change is favorable or unfavorable.

For example, an increase in revenue is generally positive, but an increase in revenue accompanied by a declining margin may signal a profitability problem.

**Important:** Your notebook calculates gross profit, not net profit. Don't label gross profit as net profit because operating expenses, marketing costs, platform fees, and other costs have not been fully accounted for.

## C. The Most Valuable Diagnostic Visuals

Some charts are more important than others because they connect directly to decisions.

### 1. Return Reasons by Category

Your notebook identifies **size issues as the leading return reason**, with 216 recorded returns across all categories. Of these, 199 are associated with Apparel.

I would use a stacked bar chart showing return reasons within each category, or a heatmap with:

- Rows: Product categories.
- Columns: Return reasons.
- Values: Number of recorded returns or percentage of returns within each category.

**Why this matters:** It helps distinguish a sizing problem from a quality problem, a product-description problem, or a logistics problem.

For example, if Apparel is dominated by sizing complaints while Electronics has more defective-product complaints, the corrective actions should be different.

### 2. Delivery Time vs. Return Rate

Your notebook reports:

| Delivery Group | Total Orders | Returned Orders | Return Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Within 7 days | 7,244 | 484 | 6.68% |
| More than 7 days | 1,505 | 187 | 12.43% |

This suggests that slower deliveries are associated with higher return rates.

I'd display the comparison as two bars with the percentage labels clearly visible.

**Business question:** Are longer delivery times associated with a higher likelihood of returns?

The chart can demonstrate an association, but it cannot establish that delivery delays cause returns. Product category, destination, and channel may also affect both metrics.

### 3. Channel Performance

Your notebook identifies Marketplace as the weakest-performing channel across the four measures examined.

| Metric | Marketplace | Mobile App | Website |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Average Discount | 19.64% | 4.40% | 4.47% |
| Gross Margin | 43.21% | 52.16% | 52.12% |
| Return Rate | 11.36% | 7.09% | 6.26% |
| Average Delivery Time | 8.42 days | 3.49 days | 4.10 days |

I would show four small comparisons rather than putting all four metrics on one shared axis. Percentages and days have different units, so combining them on a single scale would make the chart misleading.

**Business question:** Is Marketplace's performance driven by discounts, logistics, product mix, returns, or some combination of these factors?

This is a strong diagnostic section because it connects operational performance with financial outcomes.

### 4. Customer Cohort Retention

Use a heatmap with:

- Rows: Customer acquisition month.
- Columns: Month 1, Month 2, and Month 3.
- Cell values: Retention percentage.

Your notebook already contains a cohort table, making this a natural choice.

The heatmap should preserve missing values as missing, rather than showing them as zero. Recent cohorts may not have had enough time to accumulate three months of follow-up data.

**Business question:** Are newer customer cohorts returning less frequently than earlier cohorts?

This helps identify where customer retention may need attention.

# 8. Let's Pick the Design Before Coding

I would make three decisions now.

### Decision 1: Visual style

- **A. Executive dashboard:** Clean, restrained, KPI-first design. Best for a faculty presentation or founder review.
- **B. Analytics workspace:** More filters, detailed tables, and drill-downs. Best for hands-on investigation.
- **C. Hybrid — my recommendation:** Executive summary at the top, with detailed analytical sections below.

### Decision 2: Navigation

- **A. Two separate pages:** Financial Review and Operational Diagnostics.
- **B. One application with two main tabs:** Financial Review and Operational Diagnostics.
- **C. Sidebar navigation with sub-pages:** Financial Review, Product Analysis, Returns, Delivery & Payments, and Customer Retention.

I'd choose **C for navigation**, while keeping the two main business areas clearly separated.

### Decision 3: What should the dashboard emphasize?

- **A. Financial performance:** Revenue, gross profit, margin, and growth.
- **B. Operational problem-solving:** Returns, delivery, cancellations, and retention.
- **C. Balanced:** Both, with the founder's priority problem highlighted on the opening screen.

**My vote: Hybrid visual style + sidebar navigation + balanced emphasis.**

One final point: your notebook's strongest candidate for the founder's immediate action is reducing apparel returns, especially sizing-related returns. I would make that the highlighted operational insight—not force it into every chart, but give it a clear, evidence-backed callout that links the **15.61% apparel return rate** to the **199 recorded apparel size-issue returns**.

That gives the dashboard a business narrative, not just a collection of visuals.
